## Does the double-check really help?

| Comparison | Result | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Fights caught, 94.0% vs 88.0% (same 200 fights) | +6.0 points, unpaired 95% CI 0.3 to 11.8 | Interval only just excludes zero. This is the conservative unpaired test: the same fights are scored both times, so a paired test would be tighter, but the per-event outcomes for the deployed baseline were not kept to run one. |
| Robbery labelled robbery, 59.4% vs 8.3% (same 133 events) | +51.1 points, unpaired 95% CI 40.8 to 59.9 | Far from zero; the largest single gain measured. |
| Fight false alarms, 7 vs 2 over 5 h | exact conditional test p = 0.18 | Not significant on 5 h: 9 alarms in total cannot establish the size of the drop, only that it points the same way as the other settings. |
| Armed-threat false alerts, 14 vs 7 over 5 h | exact conditional test p = 0.19 | Not significant on its own; read as 'about half'. |
| Robbery false alarms, 7 vs 3 over 5 h | exact conditional test p = 0.34 | Not significant on 5 h. |

**How to say this honestly:** the robbery gain is large and clearly real (the intervals are far apart). The fights gain is smaller: under the conservative unpaired test its lower bound is only just above zero, and it would likely be clearer paired. The false-alarm reductions all point the same way (fights, robbery, weapons) but rest on 1 to 14 alarms each, so none of them is individually significant on 5 hours; more camera-hours are needed to pin down how many false alarms the check removes.

## Caveats that apply to every figure

- Recall on the real deployment cameras is unmeasured: no labelled incident has been recorded on them.
- The check's cut-offs were fitted on half the source videos and scored on the other half, but the keep rates (98% and 80%) were chosen after seeing results.
- Fight clips are spliced into street footage; the events are real held-out fights, the setting is constructed.
